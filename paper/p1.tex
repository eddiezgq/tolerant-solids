\title[Realizations of tolerant B-reps]{When does a tolerant B-rep define a solid?\\
Existence, non-existence and uniqueness of realizations}
\author{Guoqing (Eddie) Zhang}
\date{Draft, 12 November 2026. Not for circulation.}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Boundary representations in CAD kernels carry tolerances: edge curves, the boundary curves of the adjacent
faces, and vertex points do not fit together exactly, and the solid they describe is only implied. We make this
precise. A \emph{realization} of a tolerant B-rep is a PL embedding of its cell complex whose cells stay in their
tolerance zones. We prove three kinds of results.
(1) \emph{Uniqueness:} if every shell has genus at least one, the carriers of the cells are contractible, and each
shell has a product collar containing its zones, then all realizations are ambient isotopic, and so are the solids
they bound. The collar condition reduces to a distance bound below the weak feature size of a reference surface,
and for polyhedral reference surfaces we give an explicit lower bound for the weak feature size in terms of
conical radii; the key lemma is machine-checked in Lean.
(2) \emph{Existence:} under computable local hypotheses there is a realization, built either by fans in the
normal disks of the edges with cones at the vertices, or, for sharp edges with tight tolerances, by
re-intersecting the face surfaces.
(3) \emph{Non-existence:} if two planar patches cross transversally away from the shadows of all edge and vertex
zones, there is no realization.
Experiments with OpenCascade show that its validity checks do not decide any of these questions. In particular,
an ordinary sewing operation produces a wedge that passes both of OpenCascade's checks and has no realization,
and a thick pipe that passes both checks has two realizations whose solids are not homeomorphic.
\end{abstract}
