Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "When does a tolerant B-rep define a solid? Existence, non-existence and uniqueness of realizations", for publication in Computer-Aided Design as a research paper.

CAD kernels store boundary representations with vertex, edge and face tolerances, and the solid such a model describes is only implied. The manuscript makes this precise. It defines a realization as a PL embedding of the cell complex that keeps every cell in its tolerance zone, and proves:

- uniqueness up to ambient isotopy for shells of genus at least one, under a collar condition that follows from a distance bound below the weak feature size of a reference surface;
- an explicit lower bound for the weak feature size of polyhedral surfaces, with the key lemma machine-checked in Lean 4;
- two existence constructions under stated hypotheses, and a checkable non-existence criterion for crossing planar patches.

Experiments with OpenCascade 8.0.1 show that its validity checks decide neither question. An ordinary sewing operation produces a model that passes both checks and has no realization, and a model that passes both checks has two realizations whose solids are not homeomorphic. All scripts, results and the Lean formalization are publicly available.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and a preprint may be posted on arXiv. The use of generative AI in the research and writing is declared in the manuscript. There are no competing interests.
